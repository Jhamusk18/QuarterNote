Problem: retail investors following a handful of Indian stocks have to piece together each announcement from exchange PDFs, broker alerts, Screener and secondhand summaries. They cannot quickly tell what changed, how much it matters, or where to verify it. Evidence: QuarterNote’s live NSE pull for 18 companies, 29 Oct to 27 Nov 2026, returned 210 filings, of which 35 were classified high priority. Scaled to a 10-stock watchlist that is about 117 filings a month, roughly 19 material (1 in 6). ITC’s own Q1 FY27 filing warns that its revenue lines are not strictly comparable year on year, which shows why a headline number can mislead. Prototype: every update leads with one finding and a size-of-change meter, then the chart that fits the question (trends for results, a timeline for deals, sizing for legal orders), at most three key points, a Still unknown section, and one-tap sources. Every number is tagged Company said, Calculated or Not known yet. AI reads and explains; code does the maths. Left out on purpose: buy or sell calls, price targets, social features and trading. Market sources: NSE unique registered investors 13.24 crore as of 30 June 2026 (Business Today, 17 Sep 2026). About 2.1 crore new investors in FY26 (ANI / Tribune India, 1 May 2026). 7.2 crore new SIP accounts in FY26 (NSE press release, 7 June 2026). SAM assumes 5% actively track individual stocks; SOM assumes 16,000 qualified visits × 25% activation = 4,000 activated, 5% paying = 200. Competitor map: placement is my judgment from using each product, not measured data. Screener has clean tables but starts from data, not your announcement. Broker alerts link to the PDF without analysis. ChatGPT and WhatsApp tips explain but cannot be traced to a filing page.
