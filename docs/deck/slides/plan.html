Goal: prove people come back for a second results season before charging. Funnel (planning assumptions): 4,000 qualified visits from newsletters (2,000), investor communities (1,200) and referrals (800). 25% activate, meaning they review one material update for a company they follow: 1,000 users. 30% come back when the next results land: 300. 5% of activated users pay: 50. The biggest leak is visit to activation, where three of four visitors never open an update, so the first experiments focus there: land people on a real update instead of a sign-up form. Cash budget ₹25,000, so ₹25 per activated user and ₹500 per paying user. Metric tree: the north star is weekly investors who review a material update for a company they follow. It breaks into active followers × the share who open an update × the share who check the source. Guardrails: 98% of figures match the cited filing row, and a correct answer in under two minutes. First test: eight investors do comparable tasks in QuarterNote and their usual tools, alternating the order. Unit economics (assumptions): ₹199 a month minus about ₹50 AI and data cost gives ₹149 contribution. Cost to acquire a paying user is ₹25 ÷ 5% = ₹500, so payback is about 3.4 months. At 3% conversion payback is 5.6 months; at 8% it is 2.1 months. The basic update and its evidence stay free; Pro adds a larger watchlist and deeper research. Risks: QuarterNote explains and never advises, to stay on the research side of SEBI rules, with a qualified review before public launch. Exchange data rights are checked before commercial use. Uploads are made private and deletable under India’s DPDP Act. Every number links to its filing row, and accuracy is audited monthly. Next steps: December test with eight investors; January to May results-season pilot; price test in May. Each step goes ahead only if its gate is met.
