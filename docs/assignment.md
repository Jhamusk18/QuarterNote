Product Manager Assignment

Thank you for your interest in the Product Manager role. This assignment shows us how you think about products, users and markets, so we care more about your reasoning than about polish.

Part 1: Build a prototype

Pick one of the two options below.

Option A: Take an existing financial product (trading, investing, payments, lending, insurance, personal finance, wealth, etc.) and prototype the features you think it is missing.

Option B: Design your own financial app that you believe is the best fit to bring to market.

The prototype can be clickable wireframes, a Figma flow, a no-code build or a working demo of claude artifact. It should clearly show the core user journey.

Part 2: Justify your product with a written document

Back your prototype with the following sections.

Product-market fit: Who is the user, what problem are you solving, and why does it matter now? Include evidence that the problem is real.

Market study and sizing: TAM, SAM and SOM, key trends, and the growth opportunity.

Competitor analysis: Direct and indirect players, what they do well, where they fall short, and your differentiation.

Revenue roadmap: Your monetisation model, pricing logic, unit economics, and a phased path to scale.

Design choices: Why your UX, flows and feature priorities look the way they do, and what you deliberately left out.

Go-to-market strategy: How you acquire your first 1,000 users, which channels you use, and what your CAC and retention assumptions are.

Regulatory and risk considerations: Compliance requirements, data privacy and security, and the key risks to the product and your plan to manage them.

Success metrics: Your north-star metric, supporting KPIs, and how you would measure whether the product is working.

Deliverables

A prototype (link or file)

A written document or short deck (maximum 2 pages) or this explanation can be a part of the prototype as well

Relevant questions will be asked in following rounds

Evaluation criteria

Clarity of problem and user understanding

Depth and rigour of market and competitor analysis

Quality of product thinking and prioritisation

Commercial viability

Design judgment

Communication and structure

Make your assumptions explicit, and state where you used data and where you used judgment. We would rather see a sharp, well-defended product than a long list of features.

If anything is unclear, reach out. Good luck!
